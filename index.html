Physical prototype

Can Generated Points of Interest Intuitively Communicate Resource Affordances to Players?

A Unity survival game where players sail a boat between procedurally generated islands, each hiding one of three resources. My research tested whether an island's generated appearance — shape, colour, terrain and props — could intuitively signal what resource it held without explicit markers: darker, jagged islands with rocks and oil tanks signal fuel; flatter, circular islands with palm trees signal food. Tested against a control group using consistent colour-coding across a 32-participant study.

About

A bit about me

I am a UK-based Game Developer specializing in Unity, Unreal Engine and C# scripting. I recently graduated from the University of Greenwich and wrapped up a 14-week studio project as Lead Programmer on Whoever Left the Light On, successfully shipping a game to Steam with a 13-person team. I really enjoy the whole process of making games, from early-stage physical prototyping like my hexagon-grid board game CapSize to analyzing user-testing telemetry and optimizing final digital builds. Outside of development, I'm a sous chef with several years of experience leading and working within a fast-paced kitchen. I'm also a PC-building enthusiast, a Chelsea fan, and an avid Elden Ring player.
